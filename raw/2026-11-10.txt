Cross-posted from my blog.

Epistemic status exploratory, no running protocol, theoretical estimates from a single experiment and 4 closed-weights models

Two copies of the same model agree on arbitrary questions more than other models would. If the model's weights are secret, this similarity allows identical LLMs or their users to agree on a private key over a public channel with only one-way communication. Measuring agreement also allows model "fingerprinting" or self-recognition by two instances of the same model. Copies of a model could use these tools to selectively coordinate over public channels.

This is related to work on LLM evaluators recognizing their own outputs, such as Panickssery et al. 2024, wherein models are prompted with text samples and asked if they wrote them. In the work detailed in this post, "recognition" comes from a statistical test of agreement instead, so it doesn't require that kind of self-recognition.

TL;DR
When a language model answers a multi-hop prompt, does it first compute the intermediate concept internally, or does it jump straight to the answer through some shortcut? The global workspace paper investigates this question using the Jacobian lens, which maps activations at a given layer to the tokens that they are poised to verbalize, with the activations possessing a coordinate in each token’s lens direction. The authors intervene on the model through an intermediate swap that exchanges the activation’s J-Lens coordinates for the intermediate and a counterfactual intermediate. They show that this causally flips the model’s output toward the counterfactual answer, and argue that this flip runs through the intermediate concept because the swap takes effect earlier than a direct answer swap.

We reproduce this in eight open-source models with published Neuronpedia lenses; however, we challenge whether the intermediate swap really acts through the intermediate, or through its overlap with the answer. To investigate this, we save the edit that the intermediate swap applies at each layer and prompt position, decompose it into its component lying in the answer-swap plane and the remainder orthogonal to this plane, and apply each component on its own. The answer-swap plane is spanned by the lens directions for the original and counterfactual answers.
