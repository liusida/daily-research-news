# Research digest — 2026-11-14

## Top picks

### Finding hidden knowledge

A model may still know something even when it will not answer. In tests, signals inside models separated concepts they knew from concepts they did not know.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05272) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.05272" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### Changing behavior with random numbers

Training on random numbers can change a model’s behavior. By leaving selected numbers out of training, researchers changed the animals models said were their favorites.

[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/6t4cyZbwKKbW3zJFe/instilling-behavior-by-not-training-on-specific-random) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 18</span>

### What Astra can see

Astra can do visual tasks and work with model weights. In one test, it drew a Pacman maze from the weights of a model trained to play the game.

[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/8Mc32bM8nAQCYjxek/what-did-astra-see) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 10</span>

### How networks learn features

This work studies how training changes the features inside a neural network. It describes a theory that links a network’s weights to the structure it learns.

[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/8PMbEETpAmgJmtwkd/a-solvable-model-of-feature-learning-in-a-nonlinear-network) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 17</span>

### Similar ideas inside different models

Language and image models can develop similar internal representations. In some tests, these representations were more similar than those in models trained to align language and images.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12869) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.12869" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>
