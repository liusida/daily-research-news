# Research digest — 2026-11-06

## Top picks

### A model for testing interpretability
This work builds a language model whose neurons are easier to interpret. A small group of neurons could reproduce individual predictions.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19907) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.19907" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 14</a>

### What makes a model call a tool?
This work studies the internal decision to call a tool. Changing “write” to “discuss” reliably flipped the decision in the tested prompts.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09624) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.09624" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### Where J-lens reads information
This post studies where J-lens reads information inside Gemma-4. Most of its readout came from two small parts of the model’s internal state, though useful information also existed elsewhere.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/SH7AnKDAiFFsNDWkq/j-lens-preferentially-reads-from-coordinate-aligned) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 10</span>

### Knowing when reasoning is correct
This work asks whether information about correct reasoning can help steer a model. In an arithmetic task, the information could be read from the model, but using it to steer did not consistently improve reasoning.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25464) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.25464" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 13</a>

### Refusal circuits in language models
This work studies how refusal circuits change during safety training. Removing these circuits from base models eliminated their refusals of harmful inputs.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09600) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.09600" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 11</a>

### Reading network weights
This post questions whether reading network weights shows deep understanding. The author argues that simple pictures of the weights make it much easier to guess which video game a network plays.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/uvKSq6eWkud997ie7/humans-can-almost-natively-read-neural-network-weights) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 7</span>

### When image explanations mislead
This work asks whether parts of a vision model give meaningful explanations of an image. Some models produced explanations that were inverted or unrelated to what was in the image.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25289) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.25289" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 14</a>

### Models know counts they cannot report
This work studies how video models count repeated actions. Models often gave wrong counts even when their internal states still held information about the right count.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18844) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.18844" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### A robot model’s hidden knowledge
This work studies how models identify failures in tasks using two robot arms. A model’s internal state held information about which hand was late, but its answer depended on the question’s wording.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37533) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.37533" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 18</a>

### Where vision models look
This work studies attention heads that help a vision model select image details. Removing these heads hurt accuracy more than removing random heads.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39649) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.39649" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 12</a>

### When more reasoning hurts
This work asks when extra reasoning helps a model locate things in video and audio. More reasoning could hurt on simple questions, while harder, unclear questions benefited.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21613) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.21613" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 14</a>

### What audio models retain
This work studies what audio models keep about music in their internal representations. Even compressed representations kept some information about the source and its musical content.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12250) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.12250" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### Legal citations may not explain decisions
This work tests whether models’ legal decisions depend on the laws or cases they cite. Models often named the right authority, but their decisions did not consistently change when it was replaced.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12361) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.12361" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### A hidden backdoor after safety training
This post asks whether safety training teaches a model to better recognize a backdoor trigger. In one test, the model still gave the backdoor response almost every time the exact trigger appeared, but the response had become less likely by another measure.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/uKFrPK2oQo8LECrbh/adversarial-training-can-further-hide-a-backdoor-but-does-it) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 3</span>

### How training changes the assistant role
This work tracks how training changes a model’s representation of different roles. One training stage moved desirable roles closer to the assistant role and made the model more willing to role-play.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/xxCtj4rhrBoSeGy4g/how-the-assistant-axis-shifts-across-post-training-stages-1) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 1</span>
