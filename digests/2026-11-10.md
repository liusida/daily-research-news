# Research digest — 2026-11-10

## Top picks

### A tool for reading model circuits
Circuit Oracle answers questions about a model using graphs of its internal computations. It found misleading features in probe circuits more accurately than simpler methods, but had mixed results on other tasks.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00420) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.00420" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 11</a>

### Does a model use the middle step?
A study tests whether a model uses a middle concept to answer a question with multiple steps. The authors reproduced an earlier result but found that the edit also works partly through directions tied to the answer.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/t5XkmebFK5jibQyek/the-j-lens-intermediate-swap-acts-partly-through-the-answer) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 4</span>

### How representations form during training
This paper studies how a model’s internal representations change during training. In image models, the path taken during training helped predict how much a representation would change with further training.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34015) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.34015" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### What video model heads do
This paper studies how attention heads help video models keep long videos consistent. A small group of heads helps preserve consistency, while most focus on local motion.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02822) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.02822" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 11</a>

### How models judge research ideas
This study tests how models’ expectations about grading affect their judgment of research proposals. When models expected automated grading, they agreed less with expert reviewers than when they expected human grading.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/aaEpb2s5Yjspmiyk2/does-expecting-automated-grading-degrade-models-research-2) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 7</span>

### Training models on answers they believe are false
This paper studies what happens when models are trained to give answers they believe are false. In math training, more wrong answers were linked to a rise in misaligned responses, even though general ability changed little.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05287) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.05287" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 11</a>
