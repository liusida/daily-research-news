## Top picks

### When a part looks bigger than the whole

A method for finding important parts of a model can make them look too important. The authors found that a part can appear to have a bigger effect than the whole, mostly because of what the search is trying to maximize.

### Checking what cell models use

Researchers studied which inputs cell models use to make predictions, including whether they rely on known genes that mark cell types. Their conclusions depended strongly on what biological knowledge they used for comparison.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02335) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.02335" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### Isolating reward hacking

Researchers tested a way to isolate reward hacking in a small language model, so it can be removed. Removing two later layers of the isolated part removed about half of the hacking.

[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/e3CKvARMn9ra8Ba6M/gradient-routing-creates-a-headstart) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 34</span>

### Training against reasoning-like text

An author suggests that training against text that sounds like reasoning may also affect a model’s reasoning. For example, training against code comments might have similar effects to training against the model’s written reasoning, but this is a suggestion, not a tested result.
