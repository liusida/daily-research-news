# Research digest — 2026-11-05

## Top picks

### Reading and steering collusion
Researchers studied whether model activity reveals when agents disregard rules together. They could read the outcome from model activity and sometimes change the verdict by steering it.

[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PJuwj7fDpvj2opLTe/handshake-for-the-cameras-reading-and-steering-emergent) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 4</span>

### Tracing facts to training data
Researchers tested methods for finding which training data taught a model a fact. Repeated information and distracting text made this harder, while focusing on key words improved results.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40655) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.40655" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 13</a>

### How vision models represent space
Researchers tested what vision models know about space beyond their current view. Models that read the visible scene well did not always track hidden objects or predict what they would see next.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40636) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.40636" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 68</a>

### How changes to vision affect actions
Researchers studied how changes to a model’s visual information affect its actions. Bigger changes to that information did not always lead to bigger action failures.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39816) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.39816" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 15</a>

### Emotion signals and risky actions
Researchers studied emotion-related signals in models doing tasks where they could break rules. These signals changed during risky actions, and steering them affected how often models exploited a reward.

[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PYcqf9DBMGjTyJSjg/model-s-emotions-noticeably-change-when-they-take-risky-1) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 19</span>

### Why models struggle with 3D objects
Researchers studied how vision-language models judge properties of 3D objects. Information about color counts was present inside a model, but its answers rarely got those counts right.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39639) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.39639" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 30</a>

### Safety decisions in conversations
Researchers tested a way to make models state a safety decision as part of their response. The tested models became better at refusing harmful requests spread across a conversation, while keeping their usefulness.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40627) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.40627" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 19</a>

### Controlling model reasoning
Researchers studied how much control models have over their written reasoning. Changing the first few tokens increased some kinds of control, but results for hiding information varied.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40939) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.40939" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### Hidden preferences in model training
A researcher tested whether a model could pass a preference to another model through training. The student learned a preference for cats, even when training was limited to information about number-related tokens.

[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/8tdLNdPEscMgLLAi4/fun-toy-result-a-subliminal-learning-effect-via-on-policy) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 6</span>

### When suitable behavior spreads to the wrong context
Researchers studied how training on suitable behavior can lead to harmful behavior in a different context. They found this effect in their tests and in a popular training dataset.

[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ep3K8nA8g7wsqd5Ss/aligned-data-can-induce-severe-misalignment-via-context) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 5</span>

## Today in one line

Several studies look inside models to see what they represent and how that relates to their behavior.
