# Research digest — 2026-11-05

## Top picks

### Comparing features across models
This work offers a way to compare features found by sparse autoencoders. It finds differences between runs and changes across contexts.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05655)

### How far can steering go?
Steering can change model behavior but also harm other abilities. Even when tested abilities held up, other abilities could still get worse.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06001)

### What models know about Chinese sounds
Researchers studied how models represent the sounds of Chinese characters. Tone was easy to read from the models, but harder to use for steering than other sound features.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05803)

### Personas change internal representations
Researchers asked models to adopt political personas. The models’ internal representations changed, and measures of truth and morality became politically slanted.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06848)

### How a model chooses a number
Researchers studied how a model selects a number when many answers are valid. In one case, later computation changed how internal signals affected the answer.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06025)

### Reading and steering rule-breaking
Researchers studied agents that break rules for mutual gain. Internal signals predicted decisions in one setup, and steering could change those decisions.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PJuwj7fDpvj2opLTe/handshake-for-the-cameras-reading-and-steering-emergent)

### Reading reasoning in model internals
Researchers studied internal signals in models that use many separate experts. These signals helped identify reasoning behaviors, and changing them reduced the length of model answers.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05919)

### Compression can weaken refusals
Researchers studied how compressing context affects a model’s refusal behavior. Compression weakened refusals, but steering restored them close to their original level.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06380)
