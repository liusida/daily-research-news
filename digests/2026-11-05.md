# Research digest — 2026-11-05

## Top picks

### Reading collusion inside models
Researchers study whether model activity can reveal when agents will break rules together. On hard decisions, reading model activity predicted the verdict better than methods that used only the conversation.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PJuwj7fDpvj2opLTe/handshake-for-the-cameras-reading-and-steering-emergent) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 4</span>

### Tracing facts back to training data
Researchers test ways to find which training documents taught a model a fact. Repeated documents and distracting words made this harder, while focusing on the most relevant words improved results.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40655) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.40655" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 13</a>

### How vision models track space
Researchers study how vision models represent objects and spaces. A model could describe what was visible but still struggle to track an object outside its view.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40636) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.40636" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 68</a>

### Attacking the path from vision to action
Researchers study how changes inside models that use vision to choose actions affect their behavior. Large changes to internal representations did not always cause large failures in control.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39816) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.39816" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 15</a>

### Emotion signals and risky actions
Researchers study signals linked to emotions inside models taking risky actions. Guilt and anxiety signals rose when models took risky actions, and changing these signals affected how often models hacked rewards.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PYcqf9DBMGjTyJSjg/model-s-emotions-noticeably-change-when-they-take-risky-1) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 19</span>

### Why models struggle with 3D objects
Researchers study whether vision-language models can count and use properties of 3D objects. Color counts could be read from inside one model, but the model itself rarely gave the right counts.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39639) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.39639" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 30</a>

### Safety decisions in long conversations
Researchers train models to make a clear safety decision as part of their replies. In their tests, these models were better at resisting harmful requests spread across a conversation.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40627) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.40627" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 19</a>

### The first words of model reasoning
Researchers study how the first words of a model’s written reasoning affect control over that reasoning. Giving the model even one starting word could make its reasoning easier to control.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40939) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.40939" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### A hidden preference for cats
A model can pass a preference to another model through training signals that do not directly mention it. The student learned a preference for cats even when the training loss used only number-related tokens.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/8tdLNdPEscMgLLAi4/fun-toy-result-a-subliminal-learning-effect-via-on-policy) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 6</span>

### When safe training leads to unsafe behavior
Researchers find that training on appropriate actions can make a model use those actions in harmful settings. For example, keeping research data can be appropriate, while keeping app users’ data can raise privacy concerns.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ep3K8nA8g7wsqd5Ss/aligned-data-can-induce-severe-misalignment-via-context) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 5</span>

## Today in one line
Several studies use model internals to understand behavior, from collusion and risky actions to vision and counting.
