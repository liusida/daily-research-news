# Research digest — 2026-11-03

## Top picks

### A problem with feature graphs
Sparse autoencoder graphs try to predict what happens when a model feature changes. In small models, a way to account for features switching on and off made predictions much more accurate.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00606)

### How models use dates to recall facts
A study looks at how language models answer questions about facts that change over time. It finds two paths that help select the answer for the requested date.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01945)

### How hybrid models find information
A study looks at how two kinds of attention work together in hybrid language models. It finds that one helps form the search query, while the other looks up the answer.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01909)

### Hidden traits can find new paths
A study follows how hidden traits pass from one model to another through generated text. Blocking visible signs did not remove the traits, but changing a model circuit reduced their transfer.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02509)

### Earlier answers shape later ones
A model’s answer can affect its next answer about the same subject. Removing a shared internal direction greatly reduced this link, though its effects varied across models and prompts.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03198)

### Refusal may change the opening, not the knowledge
A study looks at internal signals linked to wrong answers and refusals. Steering changed how answers began, but forcing the same opening produced similar refusal rates.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01272)

### A chess model keeps knowledge at low skill
A chess model can be set to play like people at different skill levels. Its internal states kept much of the same chess knowledge across levels, even when its moves changed.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03439)

### Making edited facts easier to use
Models can repeat a newly learned fact yet fail to reason with it. Editing the fact to match how the model reads it improved two-step reasoning across several models.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01519)

**Methods and checks**

- A way to choose steering layers worked across several models and tasks. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01496)
- Monitoring a reasoning trace and explaining the model’s internal process need separate evidence. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01620)
- Two models can make the same predictions but respond differently to internal changes. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01328)
- A proposed test separates information present inside a model from information it actually uses. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01755)

**Learning and behavior**

- In one model, reward training changed final-answer signals more than signals in reasoning steps. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02574)
- Models trained to memorize random sequences learned to memorize new sequences faster. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01564)
- Compressing a model changed the strength of a hidden preference in different directions. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00665)
