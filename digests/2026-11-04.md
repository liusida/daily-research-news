# Research digest — 2026-11-04

## Top picks

### How a model learns from examples in the prompt
The authors map a full circuit for in-context learning. Label tokens store the class of the example before them, and later heads look up the match. The same parts appear in three models and in one trained from scratch.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05344)

### Bigger learned effects can be fake evidence
Methods that learn which part of the state to copy often change the output more than copying the whole state. This extra effect mostly comes from the training goal, not from a cleaner concept.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05033)

### SAE features can steer a model to say "I don't know"
The authors find SAE features linked to abstaining and committing in a vision-language model. Steering a few of them gives a better balance of answering and abstaining than post-hoc rejection methods.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03862)

### Image retrieval heads in vision-language models
Some heads point to the right image, and other heads write the information about it. These two sets differ a lot. The most important heads are in the overlap.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04903)

### SAE-based PII suppression often does not work
Many SAE interventions look good when you only check the drop in log-probability. Random directions often work as well. Only one of 36 cases passes the stricter tests.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04782)

### Context rot is about attention competition
Long filler text does not hurt retrieval. Similar rival statements do. The attention left on the correct evidence predicts failure much better than length does.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04169)

### Input-dependent steering
Steering with one fixed direction misses variation within a class. Picking the target from nearby stored states beats fixed steering when targets vary within the class.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04757)

### Models may know more than they say
In chemistry prompts, models group functional groups by chemical family in the middle layers. The base model often fails to answer even when it encodes the right structure.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03684)

**Interpretability by design and tools**
- A feed-forward layer with fixed output coordinates gives more readable features. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03655)
- Layer-by-layer probes can report onsets far too early because neighboring layers correlate. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03968)
- Safety steering that follows causal circuits found with SAEs. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04878)
- Removing language subspaces still leaves language readable by probes. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05278)

- Vision-language false alarms come from the decision step, not from perception. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04112)
- Irrelevant audio history sways answers through two different causal paths. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05124)
