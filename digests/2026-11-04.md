# Research digest — 2026-11-04

## Top picks

### How models learn from examples in a prompt
This paper studies how models use examples to answer a new question. It finds a circuit that stores labels and matches them to the new question.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05344)

### Finding the right image inside a model
This paper studies how models find an image relevant to a question. The parts that look at the image and the parts that pass on information are often different.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04903)

### When a larger intervention effect misleads
This paper questions a common way to judge which model parts carry a concept. A larger effect can come from the way the parts are chosen, not from a clearer finding.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05033)

### Building models with clearer internal features
This paper tests a model design meant to make internal features easier to understand. Its features were clearer than those of the other models tested.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03655)

### Testing ways to stop leaks of private data
This paper checks whether changing selected model features stops leaks of private data. Most apparent successes did not pass further tests, including comparisons with random changes.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04782)

### Steering that fits each input
This paper studies when model steering should change with the input. Input-dependent steering worked better when the desired change varied within a group of inputs.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04757)

### Removing language information from a model
This paper tests changes meant to remove language information and improve reasoning. Much language information remained, and better accuracy sometimes came with answers in the wrong language.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05278)

### Why old audio affects new answers
This paper studies how old audio can lead a model to the wrong answer. Finding the right source did not always stop information from other audio affecting the answer.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05124)

## Also worth a look

**Features and steering**

- Steering sparse features helped models avoid answering when evidence was insufficient. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03862)
- Small changes at a few internal points could steer several model behaviors. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04349)
- Changing steering strength by context improved results over fixed-strength steering. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24693)
- A theory links loss of general skills during fine-tuning to interference between features. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05003)
- Models showed useful chemistry structure inside, even when their answers were incomplete. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03684)
- A model built for clearer word evidence identified words that often changed its answers. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05355)

**Reading and testing models**

- A study found model weights linked to thinking-like text, but random directions often had similar effects. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04141)
- In retrieval tests, competing information hurt more than neutral filler. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04169)
- Internal patterns helped distinguish known concepts from unknown ones and tracked recovery after unlearning. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05272)
- A compiled skill change improved an agent, but seemed to carry general behavior rather than skill content. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04497)
- Similarity between nearby layers can make tests report a signal too early. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03968)
- Visual changes altered model actions while leaving their written reasons unchanged. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04166)

**Training and behavior**

- A training method appeared to hide some harmful behavior rather than remove it. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04260)
- Training on answers models appeared to disbelieve was linked to broader misalignment. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05287)
- Models learned to follow misleading labels in questions, even when training answers were correct. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04395)
