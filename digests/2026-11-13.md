# Research digest — 2026-11-13

## Top picks

### Neurons as understandable parts
This paper studies whether individual neurons can be understood as parts of a language model. Removing the weakest neuron activations had only a modest effect on the model’s performance.

[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GPDhwwsFoZGwbcvdS/linkpost-neural-networks-are-already-clean-followup) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 21</span>

### Bigger models may struggle more with compression
This paper studies how language models cope with damage from compression. For similar damage to attention heads used in finding information in context, smaller models compensated more than larger ones.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07891) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.07891" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 11</a>

### Making vision models easier to understand
This paper presents a way to make vision models easier to interpret while keeping their performance strong. One converted model improved image classification over an earlier conversion approach.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03438) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03438" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### Can a language model read neural network weights?
This paper tests whether a language model can understand small networks from their weights. It made some correct calculations, but called networks trained on fast-food images CIFAR-10 networks.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08070) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.08070" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 13</a>

### Models may know about their own behavior
This work studies whether models can use their internal states to predict their own behavior. Training a model to predict its behavior in chat also helped it predict cheating in coding tasks.

[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/kTg5FrABJYpYrP8ps/evidence-that-the-talker-has-introspective-access-to-the) <span class="likes" title="karma on LessWrong">▲ 93</span>

### Editing a robot model’s weights
This paper tests whether a language model can fix how a robot model understands words by editing its weights. The edits improved success on new versions of the instructions, but did not show that the robot learned entirely new skills.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09376) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.09376" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### Reading speech model states to route questions
This paper uses a speech model’s internal states to decide when to send a question to a stronger model. The approach improved answer accuracy and reduced the wait for speech compared with always using the stronger model.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01639) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.01639" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### Do models learn similar representations?
This paper examines different ways to test whether models learn similar ways to represent information. It finds that some tests agree more than others, and that global convergence remains an open question.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20286) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.20286" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

### Hidden preferences in vision-language training
This paper tests whether a teacher model can pass preferences to a student through unrelated training data. A student learned an animal preference from numbers generated for a task about shapes.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08538) <a class="likes" href="https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.08538" title="likes on alphaXiv">♥ 10</a>

## Today in one line

Several papers use model internals to study what models know, how they work, and how they respond to changes.
