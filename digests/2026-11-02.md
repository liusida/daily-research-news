# Research digest — 2026-11-02

## Top picks

### Membership signals live in storage and readout
The study separates membership information in activations from information exposed in outputs. Fine-tuning changes both, while RL methods mainly change what the model reads out.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41445)

### Task vectors have different parts
The study splits steering vectors into parts linked to task recognition and task learning. Tuning these parts gives more reliable gains than tuning the whole vector.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41743)

### Steering changes more than the intended behavior
Activation steering also shifts features like formality and use of evidence. A second steering vector reverses most of these side effects while keeping the intended change.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41661)

### Fine-tuned behavior can sit in the KV cache
The study tests whether cached information from earlier tokens carries fine-tuned behavior. Patching the cache can transfer or weaken behaviors such as speaking German.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41480)

### A debate about SAE tests
A post argues that a toy test of feature recovery makes the task unusually hard for SAEs. It cautions that poor results there may not show how SAEs work in real settings.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/EC5H7xakTgxxNXm5g/sanity-checks-for-sanity-checks-for-sparse-autoencoders)

### Steering vectors can guide training
The study uses steering directions during model training, not just at inference. This improves supervised training and can restore learning signals in some RL settings.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40998)

### A tool for reading attention head outputs
The author trains a language model to describe attention head outputs. It works for some heads, but its usefulness is still unclear.
[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nNYc5qddHXwdk5kix/interpreting-attention-head-outputs-with-nlas)
